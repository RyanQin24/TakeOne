# Review the shot against its purpose

Read brief, scene and direction first. Intentional reveals, occlusions, axis
changes and fragments can be valid. Do not enforce identical compositions or
a mandatory shot-size sequence.

## Story, then evidence

Follow what the audience learns, its trigger and the change at each cut. Connect
opening/ending states. Preserve identities, wardrobe, prop hand, eyelines, screen
and light direction and geography when continuous. A location change needs
separate off-camera setup.

Inspect actual solved positions, orientations, lenses and filmed frames. Check
the promised subject region and supporting people, not only a face point. Compare
achieved height/aim, actor movement, cart route and scene obstacles. Residual
errors cannot be repaired by relabelling prose. Keep setup, source filming and
edit time distinct: an end-frame preview hold supplies no new performance.

Check manual phone/light instructions. Pinhole geometry does not prove focus,
depth of field, exposure, rolling shutter, blur, stabilization crop, sound or
hardware qualification.

## Explain the observed issue and repair

Name the shot, sampled source-time range, observation, effect on intent and
smallest useful correction. Separate calculated evidence from interpretation.
Report sample coverage; never imply continuous review of actual footage.

Useful repairs give hands room, clear an eyeline, hold a reaction, reduce an
unreachable tilt or place a sign where the camera can see it. Preserve dialogue
and user facts. Call a simulation reviewable, not ready to film.

Actual-take review needs readable footage compared with saved intent. Pixel
statistics or sparse frames cannot prove acting, identity continuity, sound or
a faithful complete take. State missing evidence and the observation needed.
Never invent a universal cinematic-quality score.
